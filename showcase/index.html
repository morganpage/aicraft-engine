Draw the level. LDtk’s rules paint it.

This is a real LDtk project, edited in place. Paint into the IntGrid and the project’s own auto-layer rules choose the tiles — corners, edges, shadows and background all follow, from its own tileset.

The engine re-runs LDtk’s rules itself rather than replaying baked output: pattern matching, flips, checker and modulo spacing, opaque-tile occlusion, chance, Perlin gating and stamps. It reproduces LDtk’s own bake tile-for-tile across all 360 rules in the bundled sample projects — including which rule placed each tile.

Sprite-sheet animation.

One .json + one .png from Kenney's CC0 1-Bit Platformer defines the whole cast — player and two enemy types. The src/sprites pipeline parses an Aseprite-compatible document, compiles frame tags into animation clips, and a deterministic frame-player drives every sprite. The same deriveSpriteAnimKind picks idle/walk/jump/fall for the player and the patrolling enemies, so sprites and procedural characters share one animation vocabulary.

Camera brain.

A light Cinemachine-style camera: virtual cameras as plain config, a stateful-but-pure brain that selects by priority and blends between them, and a Celeste-style deadzone follow body with analytic dt-based convergence. Two vcams share one world: a follow cam (priority 10) and a fixed director focus (priority 20 when on, 0 when off). Toggling the focus drives a real position-and-zoom blend in both directions — selection, frozen-source blending, centre-based zoom, and interruption if you toggle mid-transition. No activeId is supplied, so priority selection is real, not an override.
